# How many bartenders do I need for my party?

A planning guide, not a rule. As a general recommendation: for up to 30 guests, one experienced bartender; for 30 to 100 guests, an experienced bartender plus support staff; for 100 or more, a team of bartenders, and we usually suggest a bar company that can supply the stock, glassware, ice and bar if you want them.

## What pushes the number up

- **A cocktail menu** takes longer per drink than beer, wine or a short list of simple mixed drinks.
- **A peak.** Guests arriving together, or a toast, create a queue that a quiet evening does not.
- **Short service windows.** A two-hour welcome drinks service is busier than a five-hour party.
- **No support.** A bartender who also fetches ice and clears glasses serves fewer drinks.
- **Layout.** Two service points halve the walking but need two people.

## The three sizes

| Guests | What we recommend | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 30 | One experienced bartender | A steady house party with a short cocktail list is comfortably served by one person |
| 30 to 100 | An experienced bartender plus support staff | Support staff keep ice, glasses and restocking moving, so the bartender can keep serving |
| 100 or more | A team of bartenders, usually through a bar company | A bar company can provide the bar, stock, glassware and ice at your request, and run the whole service |

These are planning estimates for a conversation with the bartender or bar company, and they change with your menu, arrival pattern and venue.

## How to plan it

1. Choose the drinks you will offer. See [how to plan a cocktail menu](/stories/how-to-plan-a-cocktail-menu-for-a-party).
2. Decide the busiest hour and how many guests will want a drink in it.
3. Decide who helps with ice and glasses.
4. Request the positions you need on your Event Card. Each position is invited and confirmed separately.

For a corporate event, see [staffing a product launch or drinks reception](https://ffstaff.uk/stories/staffing-a-product-launch-or-drinks-reception).
