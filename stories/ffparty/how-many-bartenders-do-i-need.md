# How many bartenders do I need for my party?

A planning guide, not a rule. The right number depends on how complicated your menu is, how many guests arrive at once, and whether anyone helps with glasses and ice. The examples below show the reasoning, with every assumption stated so you can change it.

## What pushes the number up

- **A cocktail menu** takes longer per drink than beer, wine or a short list of simple mixed drinks.
- **A peak.** Guests arriving together, or a toast, create a queue that a quiet evening does not.
- **Short service windows.** A two-hour welcome drinks service is busier than a five-hour party.
- **No support.** A bartender who also fetches ice and clears glasses serves fewer drinks.
- **Layout.** Two service points halve the walking but need two people.

## Worked examples (illustrative)

| Event | Assumptions | Planning idea |
|---|---|---|
| 30 guests, house party, 4 hours | Short cocktail list, steady arrivals, host helps with glasses | One experienced bartender |
| 50 guests, birthday, 4 hours | Cocktail list, most arrive within the first hour | One bartender plus support, or two bartenders if the list is long |
| 100 guests, reception, 3 hours | Mixed menu, drinks request at the same time | Two bartenders at least, with a glass and ice runner |

These are starting points for a conversation with the bartender you invite. They are planning estimates and are not guaranteed to suit your event.

## How to plan it

1. Choose the drinks you will offer. See [how to plan a cocktail menu](/stories/how-to-plan-a-cocktail-menu-for-a-party).
2. Decide the busiest hour and how many guests will want a drink in it.
3. Decide who helps with ice and glasses.
4. Request positions on your Event Card. Each position is invited and confirmed separately.

For a corporate event, see [staffing a product launch or drinks reception](https://ffstaff.uk/stories/staffing-a-product-launch-or-drinks-reception).
