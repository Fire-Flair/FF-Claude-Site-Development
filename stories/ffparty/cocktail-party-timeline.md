# What to Put in a Cocktail Party Timeline

A good timeline names six moments: access and setup, guest arrival, the first round, a toast or food, last orders and pack-down. Agree them with your bartender and the evening runs on a plan you both know.

## An example timeline

These times are an example. Adjust them for your own party.

| Time | Moment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 17:00 | Access and setup | The bartender arrives, meets you and sets up the bar |
| 18:30 | Guest arrival | Doors open and the bar is ready |
| 18:45 | First round | Welcome drinks go out |
| 20:00 | Toast or food | A toast, or dinner begins, with service paused or slowed |
| 22:00 | Last orders | The bartender announces the final round |
| 22:30 | Pack-down | The bar is cleared and the bartender finishes |

## Agree these with your bartender

- **Who meets them** and where they arrive.
- **Where the bar goes**, including access to water, power and ice.
- **When the final headcount or menu is due**, if that applies to your service.
- **How the evening ends**, including the last-order moment.

## Talk to the bartender directly

Alex Gagnon, founder of FireFlair, believes it works best when the bartender and the host communicate directly about the plan. A short conversation before the day answers most questions and means the bartender knows your party before they walk in.

## Match the timeline to your format

If you chose a welcome-drinks start, a cocktail hour or a bar all evening, put that window at the top of the timeline. [Welcome drinks, a cocktail hour or a bar all evening](/stories/when-to-serve-cocktails-at-a-party) covers the choice. Then pick your signature drink with [a signature cocktail for the whole party](/stories/choose-a-signature-cocktail-for-your-party).

## A checklist to send

Copy this into a message to your bartender:

1. Date, start and finish times.
2. Address and where to arrive.
3. Number of guests and how they arrive.
4. Menu and any alcohol-free drinks.
5. Who will meet you.
