# How many cocktails should I plan for my guests?

Estimate servings by thinking about how long the bar is open, how many guests will want a cocktail, and how many other drinks are available. These are planning figures to discuss with your bartender, not rules about how much anyone should drink.

## The method

1. **Guests who will want a cocktail.** Not everyone will, particularly if there is wine, beer or soft drinks.
2. **Hours of service.** Most guests have a first drink quickly, then pace themselves.
3. **Alcohol-free servings.** Include mocktails as real servings: some guests will not drink alcohol.
4. **Spread across drinks.** Divide the servings across three to five drinks on your menu.

## Worked example (illustrative)

A 40-guest party with the bar open for four hours. Say about 30 guests want cocktails, averaging 3 each: that is 90 servings. Add some extra for alcohol-free guests and busy moments and plan around 100 servings.

On FF Party, quantities are set in servings, in steps of 20. In this example you would choose 100 servings, spread across your chosen drinks. Your bartender can talk the numbers through with you.

## Things that change the number

- A longer party, but quieter pacing.
- Other drinks on offer, such as wine or beer.
- A toast, which means everyone wants a drink at once.
- The weather, since hot days mean more soft and long drinks.
