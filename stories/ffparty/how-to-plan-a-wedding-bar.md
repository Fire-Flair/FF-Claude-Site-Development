# How to plan a wedding bar

Plan the wedding bar around your timetable: what is served on arrival, with the meal, and in the evening. Then decide who provides the drinks, the bar and the staff, and check what your venue allows. Wedding bars are a good place for an experienced team, because the service has to run to time.

## Build a timetable

| Moment | What the bar usually does |
|---|---|
| Arrival drinks | Short, quick serve: a welcome drink and an alcohol-free option |
| Meal | Table service or wine, with the bar quiet |
| Toasts | Drinks ready for everyone at the same time |
| Evening | Full bar service, often the busiest period |

## Venue first

Ask your venue about corkage, whether you can bring your own drinks, who must hold the licence, and what space and power the bar can use. A temporary event notice may be needed where the venue does not already have a licence. Check current guidance for your area.

## Decide who runs each part

- **Venue bar.** Simple, but you pay the venue's prices.
- **Bartender with your drinks.** You supply drinks; see [supplying your own alcohol](/stories/supply-own-alcohol-hire-bartender).
- **Bar company.** Stock, glassware, ice and a bar are provided, and a team runs the whole evening. We usually recommend this for 100 guests or more.

## Staffing

For 30 to 100 guests, an experienced bartender plus support. For 100 or more, a team. See [how many bartenders you need](/stories/how-many-bartenders-do-i-need).

## Paying for drinks

Decide between an open bar, a limited open bar, or a cash bar. See [cash bar or open bar for a wedding](/stories/cash-bar-or-open-bar-for-a-wedding).
