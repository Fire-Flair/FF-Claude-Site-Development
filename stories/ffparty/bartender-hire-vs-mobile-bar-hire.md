# Bartender hire vs mobile bar hire

Bartender hire usually supplies the person serving drinks, with the exact tools and extras set out in the booking. Mobile bar hire may also include the bar structure, staff, equipment and drinks, depending on the package. Start with what your venue already provides, then compare the remaining responsibilities and the full price.

## Decide from your own setup

| Your situation | Likely fit | Check |
|---|---|---|
| You have a usable counter or kitchen worktop, fridge space and water | A bartender | Who brings tools, glassware and ice |
| You have the space but no bar or equipment | Staff plus equipment, or a staffed mobile bar | What the bar needs: floor space, access, power if the supplier specifies it |
| You want one team to handle everything | A full bar package | What the package includes and excludes |
| You want to supply your own drinks | A bartender, or a supplier that offers it | Venue rules and supplier terms: see [supplying your own alcohol](/stories/supply-own-alcohol-hire-bartender) |

## Why suppliers describe things differently

Some suppliers publish separate staff-only, staffed-bar and stocked-package options, and others quote around your brief. Terms such as "dry hire" are used in different ways, so read the inclusions rather than the label. See [dry bar hire explained](/stories/dry-bar-hire-staffed-bar-full-bar-service).

## When one specialist operator makes sense

A direct mobile bar company can suit a host who wants a single point of contact and a complete, tailored service. A booking platform can suit a host who wants to choose a person and see exactly what is paid for. Neither is better in every case.
