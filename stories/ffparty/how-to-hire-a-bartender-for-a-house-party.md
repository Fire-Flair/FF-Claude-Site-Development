# How to hire a bartender for a house party

For a house party, tell your bartender about the space, the access and the guest flow, and agree who supplies what. A good bartender can turn a kitchen counter or garden table into a bar, but they need to know where it is, how to get there and how many people will queue. This guide also covers garden parties and birthdays at home, because the planning is the same.

## Pick a bar spot

- A counter or table at least a metre and a half wide, away from the main walkway.
- Close to a fridge, a freezer, water and a place to wash up.
- Not in a doorway or the only route to the toilet.

## Questions to settle with your bartender

1. **Access.** Where do they park and how do they carry things in?
2. **Setup time.** Bartenders usually arrive before guests so the bar is ready.
3. **Supplies.** Who brings alcohol, ingredients, ice and glassware? See [what to provide](/stories/what-to-provide-when-hiring-a-bartender).
4. **Menu.** Three cocktails and two alcohol-free drinks is a good starting point, and it is your choice. See [planning a cocktail menu](/stories/how-to-plan-a-cocktail-menu-for-a-party).
5. **Finish.** When service stops and who clears glasses.

## Garden parties

Agree the ground and any shelter with the bartender beforehand, because a table on soft grass or in full sun needs thought. Ask about a plan B for rain, and where glasses will be collected.

## Birthdays at home

A birthday usually has a toast or a cake moment when everyone wants a drink at once. Tell your bartender the time, so they are ready. For the number of people you need, see [how many bartenders you need](/stories/how-many-bartenders-do-i-need).
