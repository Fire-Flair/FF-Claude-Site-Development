# How much ice and glassware does a cocktail party need?

Plan ice separately for serving and for chilling, and plan glassware around how many glasses are in use at once and how quickly they come back. Your bartender or bar company can confirm the numbers for your menu. The figures below are planning examples with their assumptions shown.

## Ice

- **Serving ice** goes into drinks, so it depends on how many cocktails and how many are served over ice.
- **Chilling ice** keeps bottles and mixers cold. Use a separate tub.
- **Storage.** Ice melts fast, so check freezer space and delivery time.

## Glassware

- **Types.** A short glass, a tall glass and one stemmed glass cover most menus.
- **Quantity.** Plan enough for most guests to have a glass in hand, plus spares for ones in use or being washed.
- **Reuse.** If glasses are collected and washed, you need fewer. If they are not, you need many more.
- **Alternatives.** Check the venue for restrictions on glass outdoors.

## Who provides it

It depends on the package. A bartender alone may not bring ice or glassware, while a bar company usually provides stock, glassware, ice and the bar. Use the [responsibility checklist](/stories/what-to-provide-when-hiring-a-bartender).

## Ask your supplier

1. How much ice will my menu need, and who is bringing it?
2. How many glasses are included, and are they collected and washed?
3. What happens to leftovers?
