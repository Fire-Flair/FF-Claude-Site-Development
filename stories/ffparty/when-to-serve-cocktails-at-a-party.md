# Welcome Drinks, a Cocktail Hour or a Bar All Evening?

Choose welcome drinks if you want a lively start, a cocktail hour if you want one special moment, and a bar all evening if cocktails are part of the whole party. Each works well. The right one depends on how your guests arrive and how long you want the bar open.

## The three formats

| Format | Suits | When the first round goes out | Menu length | Tell your bartender |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome drinks | Dinner or a seated meal to follow | As guests arrive | One or two signature drinks | Arrival window and when drinks finish |
| Cocktail hour | A barbecue, birthday or celebration with a focus moment | At the start of the defined window | A short menu guests can choose from | The window and the main moment, such as a toast |
| Bar all evening | Hosts who want cocktails throughout | When the first guests arrive | A fuller menu | Start, finish and any quiet points |

## Welcome drinks before dinner

Arrival drinks give guests something in hand while others come in. They work best when most people arrive close together. A signature welcome drink keeps service quick, so the bartender can greet people rather than answer long menu questions.

## A defined cocktail window

A window suits a barbecue or a birthday, where food and conversation share the evening. In my own work with house parties, a lot of the events I look after are barbecues, and a set window helps the bartender plan prep and keep the quality high. Choose the start and the end, then make a short menu for guests to pick from.

## A bar for the whole evening

If you want cocktails from arrival to the last song, tell the bartender the full time. A longer service suits a wider menu and a slower pace. Typical shifts in my examples run two to four hours, so a longer evening is worth planning early.

## Plan around your guests

- Guests arriving around one time: a first-round welcome drink lands well.
- Guests arriving across a longer window: a bar open for longer, or a window in the middle of the party.
- A larger event: one mixologist can serve a particular group or time window.

These examples are flexible. Start with the one that fits, then adjust.

## What to put in the brief

Share the start time, the finish, the guest numbers and how they will arrive, the menu, and who will meet the bartender.
