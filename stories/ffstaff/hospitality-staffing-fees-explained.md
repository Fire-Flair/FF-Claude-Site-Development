# Hospitality staffing fees and booking charges explained

Staffing is paid for in four common ways: a fixed fee per booking, an hourly mark-up, a percentage of the booking and a subscription. Compare them on the same event, and keep the fee separate from the worker's pay and from payroll costs.

## The structures

| Structure | How it works | Moves with |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed per booking** | One amount for each booking | Number of bookings |
| **Hourly** | A rate for every hour worked | Length of the shift |
| **Percentage** | A share of the booking value | Size of the booking |
| **Subscription** | A regular payment for access | Time, not use |

## A matched example (fictional)

Take a 6-hour shift with a worker paid £20 an hour, so £120 in pay.

- **Hourly agency charging £40 an hour:** £240 for the shift.
- **Fixed booking fee, as on FF Staff:** £120 pay plus a £10 booking connection fee, so £130.
- **Percentage service:** a 10% fee on £120 is £12, plus any payroll costs it adds.

Make the shift 10 hours and the hourly cost rises, while the fixed fee stays at £10. The figures are examples, not quotes from any company. Check what each service charges and who pays.

## Keep these separate

- **Fee:** what the service charges.
- **Pay:** what the worker earns.
- **Payroll and employment costs:** who handles tax, National Insurance and holiday pay depends on the arrangement.
