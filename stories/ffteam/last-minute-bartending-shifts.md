# The Last-Minute Shifts That Changed My Bartending Career

The phone would ring, an agency would tell me where they needed me, and I would go. That simple routine shaped most of my early career.

## Five agencies in London

I spent a few years in London working for around five different agencies. Each one had its own clients, so the work kept changing. One week I might be behind a hotel bar; the next, at a party where everything about the night had been planned with care.

Some of those calls sent me to five-star hotels and to the best parties I had worked. Saying yes to a short-notice booking meant meeting new teams, seeing how different venues ran their service and learning quickly. A bartender who can walk into a room, read it and settle in is a bartender people ask for again.

## What the work paid for

The agency shifts gave me the means to travel. I used the money to visit some of the best cocktail bars around the world. I watched how they welcomed guests, built their menus and trained their teams. That time away taught me that hospitality can open far more doors than most people expect from a night behind a bar.

## What I would keep

Looking back, three things made those short-notice shifts worth taking:

- A clear picture of the job before I accepted it: place, hours and what was expected.
- A team I could learn from on the night.
- A way for the next person to find me, which is where I was short for years.

That last point matters most. Every job I did well stayed in the memory of the people who were there. Almost none of it was visible to anyone who had not been in the room.
