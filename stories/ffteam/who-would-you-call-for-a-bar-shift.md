# Who Would You Call to Work a Saturday Night With You?

You would call the people who have seen you work. When a bar needs a full crew for a busy Saturday, the first names that come up belong to colleagues someone has already stood beside.

## What a colleague can tell you

A person who worked a shift next to you can speak to things a CV cannot show:

- How quickly someone prepared before service.
- Their pace when the bar was three deep.
- How they handed over at the end of the night.
- How they worked with the rest of the team.

Those are specific, checkable observations. They carry weight because they come from someone who was there.

## Three different things

It helps to keep three ideas apart:

- **A connection** means two people know each other. It does not show that you worked a particular event together.
- **A recommendation** is a colleague saying what they saw you do, from real work.
- **A check** is a separate step that confirms something factual, such as an identity or a qualification.

On FF Team, you can add people you have worked with to your profile, and the people who book you can see who you work alongside. Alex Gagnon, founder of FireFlair, has described community verification as one of the core ideas behind FF Team: the people who know your work are the best witnesses to it.

## If you are new

Everyone starts with no relationships in the trade. The quickest way to build them:

1. Work events with the same team when you can.
2. After a shift, ask the person who led it what they noticed about your work.
3. Add the people you worked with, and recommend them in return.

A first recommendation can be as simple as a lead saying you arrived prepared and stayed calm under pressure.

## Put it on your profile

Your Profile Card is the place where the people you worked with, the skills you are known for and the areas you cover come together. Share it with a colleague and they can see in one view who you are as a professional.
