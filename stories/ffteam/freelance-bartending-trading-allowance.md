# Freelance bartending and the UK trading allowance

For the 2026/27 tax year the trading allowance is £1,000. The government has announced it will increase to £3,000 from 6 April 2027, subject to the legislation. This is general information, not tax advice, and your own position depends on your circumstances.

## The current rule

If your gross self-employed income from your trade is £1,000 or less in a tax year, you can use the trading allowance and generally do not need to report it. If it is more, you normally need to register for Self Assessment, and the usual deadline is 5 October after the end of the tax year. Check the current guidance on gov.uk.

## What is changing

HMRC published a policy paper on 28 October 2026. It describes increasing the trading allowance from £1,000 to £3,000 from 6 April 2027, for the 2027 to 2028 tax year onwards, and raising the Self Assessment reporting threshold to the same level. The measure is due to be legislated in the Finance Bill 2026-27, so check that it has been enacted before you rely on it.

Until 6 April 2027, nothing has changed.

## What counts

The allowance applies to your gross income from your own trade, before costs. It covers everything you do on your own account, not each job separately. It does not apply to pay from an employer taxed through PAYE.

## Choose the allowance or your expenses

If your real costs are higher than the allowance, you may be better off deducting actual expenses instead. You cannot do both.

## Keep records

Whatever you do, keep a record of what you earn. On FF Team, your Bookings page lists your shifts, which helps you add up a year's work. FireFlair does not take tax from your pay.
