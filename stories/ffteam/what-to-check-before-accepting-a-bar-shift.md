# What to check before accepting a bar shift

Confirm the duties, the total hours including setup and clear-down, the rate, who pays you and when, how you get there and what happens if plans change. If the brief does not say, ask before you accept.

## The checklist

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Duties** | Bar service only, or also barback work, stock, glass collection or setup? |
| **Total hours** | Do paid hours include setup and finish, or only service? |
| **Arrival time and place** | Access, parking, loading, who meets you |
| **Rate and who pays** | Is the pay per hour or for the shift? Who pays, you from the booker or an agency? |
| **Payment date and method** | When, how, and whether you invoice |
| **Breaks** | When and how long |
| **Uniform and kit** | What you wear and what you bring |
| **Menu** | Cocktail list or simple service? Can you learn it in time? |
| **Support** | Is there a barback or manager? Who is your contact on the night? |
| **Transport home** | Late finishes, last trains, paid taxi |
| **Cancellation** | What happens if the event is cancelled or you cannot attend |

## Two sample briefs (fictional)

**Brief A: house party.** Saturday 7pm to midnight, home in Hertford, 40 guests, five classic cocktails supplied by host, 30 minutes setup, bring your own tools. Pay for the whole shift, paid by the host. *Questions to ask: is setup paid, who supplies ice and glassware, how do I get home?*

**Brief B: corporate drinks reception.** Thursday 6pm to 10pm, two bartenders and a barback, 120 guests, wine, beer and one signature drink, uniform provided, contact is the venue manager. Agency pays on the 15th of the following month. *Questions to ask: what is the rate, are breaks covered, what if the event ends early?*
