# How to set your freelance bartender rates

Set your rate from the hours you actually spend, the skills you bring and your costs, not from a national average. There is no single UK rate, so work out your own number and test it against the pay listed for the booking.

## Build the number

1. **Hours.** Count travel, setup, service and clearing, not just the time guests are drinking.
2. **Skills.** Classic bartending, cocktail menus and flair are different levels of work.
3. **Equipment.** Do you bring tools, a bar or ingredients?
4. **Costs.** Travel, parking, kit and insurance.
5. **Tax.** Put aside a share for tax and National Insurance.

## Worked example (fictional)

A cocktail shift: 1 hour travel, 1 hour setup, 4 hours service, 30 minutes clearing, and 1 hour travel home is 7.5 hours of your day. If you want £20 an hour for all of that, you need £150 for the booking, before costs.

## Pay on FF Team

The pay for each booking type is listed up front. In the prototype, a bartending shift of 3 hours pays £100, a cocktail shift from 4 hours pays £150, and a flair performance from 2 hours pays £200. If you sign up for a booking, you can talk directly with the client.
