# FireFlair Content Strategy

Version 1.0, approved by Alex on 8 November 2026. Living document: the rules change as the marketing strategy develops. Update this file, bump the version, and note what changed.

Applies to FF Party (FFParty.uk), FF Staff (the business platform) and FF Team (FFTeam.uk): website copy, homepage content, profile and package descriptions, package pages, FF Stories, marketing campaigns and booking interfaces.

Prices, fees and costs are deliberately not written in this document.

---

## The ten rules

1. **Positive language, always.** Every sentence on the site creates a positive impression. Use words of enjoyment, opportunity, confidence, celebration, freedom, excitement, achievement, connection and possibility. Avoid negative vocabulary, even when describing a solution ("Planning feels complex" becomes "Your favourite people. Your perfect celebration."). The glance test: someone scanning the page meets attractive, positive words throughout.
2. **Sell the experience, not the transaction.** Describe what the customer gains and how the occasion will feel. Features support the emotional proposition and never dominate it.
3. **Fewer words, one purpose per section.** Every sentence earns its place. A strong heading and one sentence often replace a paragraph. Each section does one job: inspire, explain, establish value, build confidence or encourage action. Merge or remove sections that repeat each other. Detail lives on dedicated pages and appears when the visitor asks for it.
4. **Premium, aspirational and approachable.** Confident, warm, contemporary and sophisticated. No aggressive sales language, exaggerated promises, artificial urgency, generic corporate phrases or cheap-sounding promotions.
5. **Build emotional value, then show the price plainly.** Lead with the experience and what is included. Every price is listed and is never negotiated. Prices are not repeated in marketing copy. Never manufacture savings, suggest an unverified bargain or use pressure.
6. **Consistent terminology.**
   - The company is FireFlair Ltd. The consumer brand is FF Party. The business platform is FF Staff. The professional platform is FF Team.
   - FireFlair Packages are the flagship offering and the number one priority. Where "Our Packages" appears it means FireFlair Packages.
   - Company packages (packages listed by companies on the site) are not the focus at launch and are not renamed. Context makes the difference clear.
   - The payment taken at booking is always called a deposit. Never a booking charge, service fee or administration fee.
   - "FF Business" and "FireFlair Business" are retired for good. FF Staff is the name.
   - Use consistent capitalisation.
7. **Clarity wins at the moment of commitment.** Payment, invitation, review, invoice and confirmation screens are plain and exact. The Event Invoice and the FireFlair Invoice are the complete, clear statement of what happens, and each carries its own terms and conditions (one set for company bookings, one for FireFlair bookings). Marketing language does not go on these screens.
8. **Positive persuasion, used naturally.** Outcome-focused storytelling, emotion-led framing and low-pressure calls to action. Statements are usually stronger than questions. No negative-framed sales techniques, no scripted pressure, no repeated rhetorical questions. The "ask for a no" technique is not used.
9. **Match the message to the audience and the journey.** Each site has its own voice (below). A homepage creates excitement, a package page establishes value, a practical guide delivers useful information, a booking page makes the next action clear, and a confirmation states the true booking status.
10. **Inspire action while preserving clarity.** Clear, relevant calls to action. Never invent services, package contents, booking guarantees, prices, availability, customer reviews or benefits.

## Where positivity and facts meet

Wording follows the format:

- **Cards and buttons deliver facts.** They are direct and built for speed and ease of understanding. They carry the booking information, and the booking process presents the facts in the right place and the right order. Facts are stated once, where they are needed, and not restated elsewhere.
- **Sentences are positive and exciting.** Anywhere the site uses sentences (headings, introductions, descriptions, stories), the wording is positive and exciting.
- **Exact wording lives in the documents.** The precise rules sit on the Event Invoice, the FireFlair Invoice and the two sets of terms and conditions. Marketing copy does not repeat them.

## The FireFlair Booking Guarantee

The refund protection is a selling point, not a rules explanation. It is presented as a benefit: book with confidence, because a booking that cannot be confirmed is fully refunded. The exact conditions appear only in the FireFlair Invoice and the FireFlair terms and conditions.

## Three audiences, three voices

The three sites are written for three different people. The voice differs by site; the ten rules apply to all.

### FF Party: emotion, celebration and desire
- Audience: hosts, mostly women around 30 to 60, planning celebrations of about 0 to 30 guests.
- Feeling: "I want my party to feel like this."
- Themes: celebration, friendship, memories, hosting, personalisation, effortless elegance.
- Lead with the experience and the FireFlair Packages. Do not present FF Party as a staff directory or a marketplace.
- No age or gender cues in the copy. Avoid trade jargon.

### FF Staff: direct, efficient and confident
- Audience: business customers.
- Feeling: "This is a straightforward way to find the people I need."
- Themes: professionalism, speed, efficiency, reliability, flexibility, clear booking arrangements.
- Short sentences, practical benefits, minimal steps. Emotion is used selectively.

### FF Team: energy, ambition and opportunity
- Audience: hospitality professionals, about 18 to 30.
- Feeling: "This is where I can build my reputation and find exciting opportunities."
- Themes: independence, opportunity, achievement, community, professional pride, skills, recognition.
- Energetic, modern and motivational. Not a recruitment agency. Keep profile-building instructions brief.

## Front doors and the homepage

- **The homepage is for excitement.** Its job is to get a visitor to make a purchase.
- **Front doors are for search.** FF Stories, location pages, cocktail pages, service pages and profile pages (with their FireFlair Introductions) are front doors. Their job is to bring visitors to the homepage. They combine clear descriptive headings with a little excitement and keep the search terms. We do not push visitors from the homepage out to front doors.
- **FFAI** is a data-entry assistant for launch (called "FFAI data entry" where it appears). It is not a chatbot at launch and does not discuss prices.

## Homepage pillars (FF Party)

The four pillars match the four main pages of the site, so the continuity stays. The first pillar leads with FireFlair Packages, with less emphasis on community. Each pillar opens with what the visitor is looking for, then presents the solution. Pillars present solutions, never problems.

## Record keeping
