## Stage C: Proposed structure

### 3.1 Hero: which direction converts best
| Option | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| "You've planned the perfect party. Now enjoy it." | Speaks directly to the host's real wish (to enjoy their own party) | Assumes the visitor has already planned it |
| **"Your party. Your people. Your moment."** | Works at any stage of planning; short and confident; echoes the pillars (party, people, moment) | Needs the sub-line to name the offering |
| "Make every celebration extraordinary." | Aspirational | Generic; could be any events brand |
| "Great parties deserve great people." | Clear link to the professionals | Puts people ahead of packages |

**Recommendation: "Your party. Your people. Your moment."** with a sub-line that names the flagship, and keep "Now enjoy it." for the closing call to action so the strongest line is not wasted.

- Sub-line: "FireFlair Packages bring professional bartenders and cocktails to your celebration."
- Button: **Explore FireFlair Packages** (replaces "Plan your event" in the hero; "Plan your event" moves into the Booking pillar).
- The sub-line also keeps the descriptive words (bartenders, cocktails, celebration) that the homepage still needs.

### Homepage hierarchy (the smallest set that works)
1. **Hero**: headline, sub-line, one button.
2. **Pillar 1, FireFlair Packages** (open first): associations then solutions, the package carousel, the Booking Guarantee line.
3. **Pillar 2, Network**: the people behind the packages; trust; featured people; reviews; bar companies below.
4. **Pillar 3, Booking**: three steps, the Guarantee, occasions.
5. **Pillar 4, Your Party**: preferences, favourites, rebook.
6. **One guide question**: "What are you celebrating?"
7. **FF Stories**: three cards.
8. **The FireFlair Booking Guarantee band** (coin).
9. **Closing call to action**: "Now enjoy it."

Removed from the homepage: ring diagram, philosophy block, founding row, three of the four how-it-works versions, the service list and SEO paragraph, the card journey, the hero search.

### Pillar names
The pillars match the four main pages, so continuity stays: **FireFlair Packages** (Home), **Network**, **Booking**, **Your Party**. Short labels follow Rule 3 and make the first pillar the flagship. The subtitle idea "Party at Home" can sit under Pillar 1 if you like it.

### 4. FireFlair Packages page strategy
- **Landing page:** Pillar 1 opens as the FireFlair Packages landing page. The hub already supports a page address per pillar, so no new top-level navigation item is added and the established information architecture is preserved. If you want a visible Packages link between Network and Booking, that is a navigation change and needs your explicit approval.
- **Individual package pages:** one page per package, reached from its card. Cards link to pages; the homepage does not carry the detail.
- **Where packages appear:** homepage (Pillar 1), the Booking pillar (package choice), Network (each professional's page offers the packages they deliver), FF Stories (a natural link at the end of relevant stories) and the front-door service pages.

**Reusable package page structure**
1. Emotional headline
2. A short description of the experience
3. What it includes (from the specification only)
4. Who it is ideal for
5. Personalisation, where supported
6. Price, from the package card, shown as it is. The booking summary and invoice carry the deposit detail.
7. How booking works: the three steps
8. Call to action: "Choose this package"
9. Related FF Stories

### Copy changes and structural changes

| Copy only (no developer work) | Structural (developer work) |
|---|---|
| Hero text, pillar headings, paragraphs and card text | Move Pillar 1 content and reorder pillars |

## Part 5: FF Stories

### Where Stories stand
The repository already holds 29 FF Party articles (plus 18 for FF Team and 18 for FF Staff, per `stories/INVENTORY.md`). Almost all of the Party set is **practical**: how many bartenders, how far ahead to book, what to provide, comparisons, location guides, deposits and refunds. That is good search coverage. The **inspirational** category is largely missing, and it is the category that sells the experience.

### Two categories
**Practical (existing, keep and polish)**: how to book a bartender on FF Party, how many bartenders do I need, how far in advance to book, what to provide when hiring a bartender, planning a cocktail menu, how many cocktails for a party, how to hire a bartender in London and Hertfordshire, bartender vs mobile bar hire, bartender vs mixologist vs flair bartender, supplying your own alcohol, and the booking-terms article. Check each for accuracy against Content Strategy v1.0.

**Inspirational (new, proposed)**: each opens with a relatable positive idea, helps the reader picture the evening, gives genuinely useful advice, and ends with a natural link to one package.

| Proposed story | Opens with | Links to |
|---|---|---|
| The art of hosting an unforgettable house party | The moment guests walk in and the drinks are already flowing | Bartending Package |
| Making birthdays feel extraordinary | The detail people remember years later | Mixology Package |
| A cocktail experience at home | Your kitchen as the best bar in town | Mixology Package (differentiate from the existing "cocktail masterclass at home") |
| Small parties with big personalities | Twelve guests, one unforgettable night | Flair Package |
| Ideas for memorable hen parties | A morning of planning that becomes a night of laughter | Masterclass Package (differentiate from the existing "bartender or masterclass for a hen party" decision guide) |
| Setting the atmosphere | Light, sound and a drink in hand | Entertainment packages |

### Rules for writing them
- Do not follow one template every time.
- No prolonged sales pitches; one natural package link per story.
- Internal links: each story links to its package, the related practical story, the matching location or service front door, and the booking entry. Package pages link back to their stories.
- Avoid overlapping search intent. Three existing articles sit close together on masterclasses and hen parties; the new ones must take a different angle (inspiration, not the buying decision).

### Research to do before the editorial programme is approved (no figures invented here)
Research-backed: none yet in this document. To research: what drives hosts to hire help for small celebrations; how people search for home cocktail experiences; premium hospitality positioning; the objections hosts raise about hiring a professional. Assumptions (not facts): that hosts care most about enjoying their own event; that visuals and a person's name matter more than service lists. These stay assumptions until researched.

### Items for you
- Some existing cost and refund articles carry fee figures, agreed on 7 November. Your latest direction is that prices are listed rather than repeated and that I am not involved in price discussions, so these articles need your call on whether the figures stay. I have not touched them.
- P05 (bartenders for 30, 50, 100 guests) still waits for your review of the size bands.

## Part 6: FF Staff and FF Team (recommendations only, not implemented)

### FF Staff
- **Hero:** "Welcome to FireFlair" is generic. Use a headline that names the outcome, for example "Find the right people for your event." and keep "Find staff. Book staff." as the short sub-line.
- **Pillars:** Tighten "Our community / Hospitality, brought together" into a practical benefit ("Reliable people, ready to book"). "From one shift to a festival" is already strong and direct.
- **Style:** short sentences, facts first, fewer steps. No lifestyle copy.
- **Company Card pillar:** "A company card people trust" works; keep.

### FF Team
- **Hero:** keep the energy of "Get seen. Get booked." and make it specific to FF Team, replacing "Welcome to FireFlair".
- **Pillars:** "Your craft, on one card" is good. "Meet the people behind great events" could lean harder on recognition and reputation.
- **Style:** energetic, short instructions during profile creation, nothing that sounds like an agency.

## Decisions needed before Stage E

1. **Package names.** Rename "Service Card" and the "… Service" titles to "… Package" (Bartending, Mixology, Masterclass, Flair, Full), and leave the Staffing Service off the FF Party homepage?
2. **Booking Guarantee wording.** "Confirmed, or refunded in full." Is this right when an event is only partly staffed, which the terms handle case by case? Or should the line be "Not confirmed? Fully refunded."? (The first is more positive.)
3. **Own-alcohol package.** Which package fits "I have my drinks; I want a cocktail menu and a bartender with equipment"? Does it include equipment?
4. **Masterclass.** Is "design your own recipes" part of the package?
5. **Confirmation timing.** The homepage says "confirmed within 24 hours". In practice the first-choice professional has 24 hours to answer. Do we say anything about the wider confirmation window in marketing, or only on the invoice and terms?
6. **Hero.** Approve "Your party. Your people. Your moment." with "Now enjoy it." as the closing line?
7. **Hero search.** Remove it from the FF Party hero (Network keeps search)?
8. **Founding row.** Move the founding member row and the philosophy block off the FF Party homepage?
